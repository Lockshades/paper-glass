# Vision and Product Constitution

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Accepted baseline  
**Last updated:** 2026-07-27

## Mission

Help students prepare with confidence by making high-quality exam practice, useful explanations, and evidence-based revision accessible in one trustworthy learning system.

## Vision

Build educational infrastructure that improves the way students prepare for high-stakes examinations: realistic practice for students, reliable content for educators, measurable learning outcomes for institutions, and a sustainable ecosystem for the people who create and verify academic material.

## The first product

The first product is a student-facing web application. A student can select an examination and subject, take a realistic computer-based practice test, review the result, understand mistakes with AI-assisted explanations, identify weak topics, and return to a targeted study plan.

The long-term platform may contain:

1. CBT examination platform;
2. verified question and explanation library;
3. AI tutor;
4. analytics and recommendation engine;
5. academic contributor and reviewer workflows;
6. institutional and tutorial-centre tools.

These are related layers, not one undifferentiated first release.

## Initial market hypothesis

The strongest initial hypothesis from the source material is students preparing for Nigerian examinations, beginning with a JAMB-style CBT experience and expanding to WAEC, NECO, and other examination bodies after the core workflow is validated.

This is a hypothesis, not a permanent scope decision. It must be validated with students, tutors, and examination-specific requirements.

## Problem

Students often prepare using fragmented question banks, static answer keys, unreliable explanations, and practice environments that do not resemble the real examination. They may know their score without understanding why they are weak, what to study next, or whether the content itself can be trusted.

## Product promise

ExamPilot should make the loop below feel clear and repeatable:

```text
Practice
  → Understand
  → Diagnose
  → Revise
  → Practice again
```

## Principles

### VSN-001 — Trust before novelty

Where educational correctness matters, verified content outranks unlimited AI generation.

### VSN-002 — Practice should resemble the real task

The examination interface should reduce avoidable cognitive load by behaving like the examination format the student is preparing for.

### VSN-003 — AI explains; it does not quietly become the answer key

AI may explain, adapt, classify, and recommend. The published answer key must have a controlled provenance and review state.

### VSN-004 — Every score should teach something

A result page is incomplete if it only reports marks. It should help the student understand strengths, weaknesses, time use, and next actions.

### VSN-005 — Build the smallest learning loop first

The first milestone should validate repeated student value before expanding into a marketplace, complex institutional tooling, or a multi-million-user architecture.

### VSN-006 — The company should remember why

Important decisions, experiments, and reversals belong in the Blueprint, not only in conversation history or code.

## Non-negotiables

- No fabricated claims of academic accuracy.
- No silent use of AI as an authoritative answer key.
- No collection of student data without a defined purpose and retention rule.
- No gamification that obscures learning progress or pressures vulnerable students.
- No launch without a way to report questionable content.

## Success at the first milestone

The first milestone is successful when a real student can:

1. start a practice session quickly;
2. complete or resume it without losing answers;
3. receive a trustworthy result;
4. understand at least one mistake;
5. see a useful next study action;
6. choose to return because the loop created learning value.

## Open decisions

- Final brand name and positioning.
- Exact first examination and subject combination.
- Free, freemium, subscription, or institution-led initial commercial model.
- Whether the first release requires accounts or supports a guest trial.
- Which academic reviewers and source materials will establish the initial content standard.
